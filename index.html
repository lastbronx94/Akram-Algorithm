How the score is calculated

VIS = dopamine + dobutamine + 100 × epinephrine + 100 × norepinephrine + 10 × milrinone (all in µg/kg/min) + 10,000 × vasopressin (U/kg/min). Gaies et al., Pediatr Crit Care Med 2010.

Levosimendan is not in the original score. It is added here as 50 × levosimendan (µg/kg/min), as in the extended score (Favia et al., 2013; Belletti et al., 2021).

A score of 20 or more is a common yardstick for high support. No pediatric feeding guideline gives a dose cut-off: enteral nutrition is held for uncontrolled shock (doses rising, lactate rising, perfusion targets not met), not for the number alone. A stable or falling dose means the shock is controlled.

High risk of aspiration: what to do
  • Keep the head of the bed raised 30° to 45°, unless contraindicated.
  • Consider a transpyloric (nasoduodenal or nasojejunal) tube.
  • Give the feed as a continuous infusion instead of boluses. Transpyloric feeds are always continuous.
  • Treat what can be treated: reflux, vomiting, delayed gastric emptying (consider a promotility agent), deep sedation.

Risk: depressed cough or gag, altered mental status, delayed gastric emptying, significant reflux or vomiting, severe bronchospasm, prone positioning, or head of bed cannot be raised above 30°.

This worksheet applies the unit’s stepwise enteral nutrition algorithm: Schofield weight–height equation, energy target by phase of illness, catch-up target with the FAO/WHO/UNU requirement, protein by age, fixed advancement steps, clinical definition of intolerance, fixed fasting times and risk-based timing of parenteral nutrition. Weight-for-age z-scores use the WHO Child Growth Standards below 5 years and the CDC 2000 growth charts from 5 years. The vasoactive-inotropic score is added as a bedside aid for judging cardiac support. It supports the treating team’s decision and does not replace clinical judgment; check every figure before prescribing. Nothing you enter is saved or sent anywhere.
